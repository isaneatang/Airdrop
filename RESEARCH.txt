PART 0. WHAT THIS DOCUMENT IS
================================================================================

The blueprint was a specification, not code. This document records:

  (a) the structure it specified, described precisely enough to audit;
  (b) what was found wrong with it, ranked by severity, with proof;
  (c) what was changed, and why each change is the change it is;
  (d) evidence that the fixes hold.

One finding is critical: the specified cancellation logic drains the contract.
It is reproduced as a passing test against a model of the original design, so
the claim is demonstrated rather than asserted. See PART 2, finding C-1.

Method: the blueprint was read against the pinned OpenZeppelin 5.6.1 source
rather than against recollection of its API. Three claims in the blueprint were
checked directly against that source and all three held (PART 6.4).


================================================================================
PART 1. THE EXISTING STRUCTURE, AS SPECIFIED
================================================================================

1.1 THESIS

"Chain as witness, not custodian." The contract holds funds and enforces a
schedule. It never claims title, never takes custody in the legal sense, and
never trusts an off-chain actor to decide who gets paid.

Operationally this means: no owner, no pause, no upgrade path, no sweep, no
rescue function. Every one of those is an escape hatch, and an escape hatch is
the trust the design exists to remove.

1.2 SHAPE

One pure math library feeding two products with deliberately opposite trust
postures:

    VestingMath  (pure, stateless, no external calls)
         |
         +-- PaymentStream        1 to 1,  revocable at creator's option
         +-- MerkleVestedAirdrop  1 to N,  non-revocable, hard-coded

                       PaymentStream        MerkleVestedAirdrop
    Shape              1 sender, 1 recip    1 funder, N recipients
    Funder gas         O(1) per stream      O(1) total, any N
    Recipient list     n/a                  Merkle root, 32 bytes on chain
    Revocable          yes, set at create   no, not configurable
    Use case           salary, contractor   token launch, community drop

1.3 WHY REVOCABILITY DIFFERS

This asymmetry is the strongest idea in the blueprint and survives review intact.

Streams fund ongoing work. If delivery stops in week two, the payer must be able
to recover the unstreamed remainder. Cancellation splits at the current
timestamp: accrued to the recipient, remainder to the sender.

Airdrops are different in kind. If the issuer can claw back, recipients hold a
promise rather than an entitlement, which is strictly worse than a plain
transfer because it adds gas and a claim flow to something that still requires
trusting the issuer. The credible commitment is the product.

Critically: making it a toggle does not give recipients a choice. It lets every
issuer pick the weaker version, and it destroys the guarantee for everyone
downstream, because a claimant can no longer tell from the contract type alone
whether their allocation is real. Non-revocability has to be structural to mean
anything.

1.4 THE ACCRUAL ENGINE, AS SPECIFIED

    function vestedAmount(uint256 total, uint64 start, uint64 cliff,
                          uint64 end, uint64 timestamp)
        internal pure returns (uint256)
    {
        if (timestamp < cliff)  return 0;
        if (timestamp >= end)   return total;
        unchecked {
            // end > start guaranteed by constructor validation
            return (total * (timestamp - start)) / (end - start);
        }
    }

Five invariants were stated: monotonic, bounded by total, exact at and past end,
zero before cliff, no overflow. All five are correct as goals. The terminal
exactness branch is the important one: returning `total` directly rather than
computing it is what releases the dust that integer division truncates away over
the life of the schedule. Computing the final payout as a fraction strands funds.

1.5 PAYMENTSTREAM, AS SPECIFIED

State: a Stream struct (sender, recipient, token, deposit, withdrawn, start,
cliff, end, revocable, cancelled) in a mapping, keyed by a monotonic counter.

The counter is right and the blueprint says why: hash-derived IDs collide when
the same sender opens two identical streams, and the second silently overwrites
the first.

Three entrypoints: create (validate, pull tokens measuring the balance delta,
store, emit), withdraw (claimable = vested minus withdrawn, effects before
interactions), cancel (split at now, assert the accounting, freeze, transfer
both legs).

1.6 MERKLEVESTEDAIRDROP, AS SPECIFIED

All schedule parameters immutable. No owner, no pause, no upgrade. Two mappings:
a claimed bitmap and a cumulative claimed amount, both keyed by leaf index.

Leaf encoding:

    bytes32 leaf = keccak256(bytes.concat(keccak256(
        abi.encode(index, account, amount))));

The double hash is correct and the reasoning given is correct: with a single
hash, a 64-byte leaf preimage is indistinguishable from the concatenation of two
sorted internal nodes, so an attacker can present an internal node as a leaf and
forge a claim. Hashing twice puts leaves in a different preimage domain from
internal nodes. Using abi.encode rather than abi.encodePacked is also correct,
because packed encoding admits ambiguous preimages across adjacent fields.

Funding is two-step by design: deploy, transfer tokens in, then call activate(),
which checks the balance covers totalAllocated. This avoids an approve and
transferFrom dance in the constructor and makes underfunding loud instead of
silently breaking the last claimant.

Payout always goes to the account committed in the leaf, never to msg.sender.
This is the single best decision in the airdrop design and PART 2 explains why
it also closes the front-running surface for free.

1.7 WHAT THE BLUEPRINT GOT RIGHT

Recorded plainly, because a review that only lists faults misrepresents the
document:

  - Library isolated as the single point of failure and gated first in the
    build order.
  - Terminal exactness, with the dust reasoning spelled out.
  - Double-hashed leaves with abi.encode.
  - Pay the leaf's account, never the caller.
  - Balance-delta measurement on intake for fee-on-transfer tokens.
  - SafeERC20 rather than hand-rolled low-level calls, with the return-bomb
    reasoning attached.
  - An explicit unsupported list instead of an admin bypass "to help".
  - Refusal to add sweep or recoverTokens, and the reason given.
  - An adversarial test matrix, including internal-node-as-leaf.
  - Honest positioning: Sablier proved streaming in 2019, Uniswap proved Merkle
    claims in 2020, and the pitch does not claim novelty.

PART 2. FINDINGS
================================================================================

Ranked by severity. Each names the fix and where it landed.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C-1  CRITICAL. Cancellation drains the contract.
     blueprint line 137. Fixed in src/PaymentStream.sol cancel().
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The spec says:

    "Mark cancelled, set end = block.timestamp so future accrual is frozen"

It does the exact opposite. VestingMath returns `total` for every timestamp at
or past `end`. Moving `end` into the past therefore makes the FULL deposit read
as vested, forever after.

Exploit, with numbers:

  1. Sender opens a 1000-token stream, start = now, end = now + 30 days.
  2. At day 10 the sender cancels. Recipient is paid ~333, sender refunded ~667.
     `end` is now day 10. The split itself was correct.
  3. At day 11 the recipient calls withdraw. `timestamp >= end`, so vested reads
     as the full 1000. claimable = 1000 - 333 = 667.
  4. That 667 was already refunded to the sender. The transfer still succeeds,
     paid out of OTHER streams' deposits. The contract is now insolvent and the
     last stream to withdraw reverts.

Worse variant: cancel a stream whose `start` is in the future. Now `end < start`,
inverting the schedule. Same drain, plus `end - start` underflows inside the
unchecked block if the division branch is ever reached.

The fix is not to freeze `end` more carefully. It is to not touch `end` at all.
Cancellation already settles both legs in the same call, so nothing legitimate
remains to withdraw afterwards. The implemented cancel():

    s.cancelled = true;
    s.deposit   = vested;   // schedule now tops out at what actually accrued
    s.withdrawn = vested;   // and it has all been delivered

With deposit == withdrawn, claimable is structurally zero at every future
timestamp including past the original `end`. withdraw() additionally reverts
with StreamIsCancelled, so the failure is legible rather than a silent zero.

PROOF: test/CancelRegression.t.sol contains LegacyCancelStream, a minimal model
of the original logic. Four tests:

  test_Legacy_CancelThenWithdraw_OverReleasesFullDeposit
      passes, i.e. it demonstrates the original design re-exposes the sender's
      refunded remainder one second after cancellation.
  test_Fixed_CancelThenWithdraw_Reverts
      claimable is 0 immediately after, withdraw reverts, and still 0 a year
      past the original end.
  test_Fixed_CancellationCannotTouchAnotherStreamsFunds
      an unrelated stream still pays out in full after a cancellation.
  test_Fixed_CancelBeforeStart_RefundsAllAndStaysSettled
      the inverted-schedule variant: full refund, nothing accrued, contract
      balance zero, stays settled past the original end.

C-2  HIGH. The spec contradicts itself on what cancel() pays.
     blueprint line 138 vs line 256. Resolved.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Line 138: "then transfer both legs"            -> cancel pays the recipient
  Line 256: "Withdraw after cancel -> only the   -> cancel does NOT pay the
            frozen accrued amount"                  recipient; they withdraw later

These are two different contracts. An implementer picks one and half the test
matrix is then wrong.

RESOLVED as pay-both-legs-at-cancel. It is atomic, it leaves one fewer state to
reason about, and it removes the entire post-cancel withdraw surface that
produced C-1. The corresponding matrix row is now "withdraw after cancel
reverts", implemented as test_Withdraw_OnCancelledStreamReverts.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C-3  HIGH. The library is unchecked but validates nothing itself.
     blueprint lines 64 to 67. Fixed in src/libraries/VestingMath.sol.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The blueprint calls VestingMath "the single point of failure" (line 45) and then
makes it the one component that trusts its inputs. Both subtractions sit inside
`unchecked` and their safety rests on a comment plus the caller having validated
`cliff >= start` and `end > start`. Because the function is `internal pure`, any
future caller (a new contract, a view helper, a script) gets silent wraparound
instead of a revert. A wrapped `timestamp - start` produces an astronomically
large multiplier.

Separately, `total` was typed uint256 while invariant 5 required it to be at most
uint128 max. The type system should carry that, not prose.

FIX, and this is the part worth reading, because it costs nothing:

    if (timestamp >= end)   return total;
    if (timestamp <= start) return 0;
    if (timestamp < cliff)  return 0;
    unchecked { return (uint256(total) * (timestamp - start)) / (end - start); }

Reordering the guards makes the function total. To reach the unchecked block a
call must pass `timestamp < end` AND `timestamp > start`, which together prove
`start < timestamp < end` and therefore `end > start`. Both subtractions are now
underflow-free for EVERY possible argument, including a corrupt or unvalidated
schedule. No revert added, no validation duplicated on the read path, no reliance
on the caller.

`total` is now uint128, so the overflow bound is enforced by the compiler:
total < 2^128 and (timestamp - start) < (end - start) <= 2^64, so the product is
bounded by 2^192 and cannot overflow uint256. The blueprint's arithmetic on this
point was correct; only its enforcement was prose.

A separate `validateSchedule(start, cliff, end)` is called at creation by both
contracts. It exists to fail loudly and early on a useless schedule, not to make
vestedAmount safe, and it also enforces the minimum-duration floor from C-9.

PROOF: testFuzz_TotalFunctionOnUnvalidatedSchedules deliberately does NOT
normalise its inputs. `end` may be at or below `start`, `cliff` may sit outside
the range. 4096 runs, bound holds, no revert. That is precisely the input space
the original unchecked block wrapped on.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C-4  HIGH. The on-chain accounting assert is a tautology.
     blueprint line 136. Replaced.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The spec asks for this to be encoded on-chain rather than only tested:

    recipientOwed + senderRefund + withdrawn == deposit

Substituting the definitions given two lines above:

    (vested - withdrawn) + (deposit - vested) + withdrawn == deposit
     ->  deposit == deposit

It reduces to an identity. It can only fail on overflow, which 0.8.x already
catches. It burns gas and, worse, creates the impression that cancellation
accounting is verified on-chain when nothing is being verified. It would not
have caught C-1.

REPLACED with a check that can actually fail: `vested > deposit` reverts with
AccrualExceedsDeposit (and AccrualExceedsAllocation in the airdrop). That is
invariant 2 of the blueprint's own list, enforced at the point of use. It is
unreachable given a correct VestingMath, which is the point: it halts rather
than paying one stream out of another stream's balance if the shared library
ever regresses.

Note on ordering, which matters: the bound is checked on the full uint256 BEFORE
narrowing to uint128. Checking after the cast would let a hypothetical
over-accrual truncate into a small value that slips past the guard. See
PaymentStream._vested().

A per-token liability counter was considered and rejected. It would be a
stronger invariant, but decrementing it by the nominal payout while a
fee-on-transfer token reduces the balance by payout plus fee would brick the
contract for exactly the token class the stream is supposed to support.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C-5  MEDIUM. Fee-on-transfer is unsupportable in the airdrop, but the token
     table implies otherwise. blueprint line 198. Documented and split.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The mitigation "measure balanceAfter - balanceBefore on intake" works for
PaymentStream, where the deposit is recorded after the transfer lands. It cannot
work for MerkleVestedAirdrop: per-recipient amounts are baked into the Merkle
leaves before deployment and totalAllocated is immutable. If funding loses 2% to
a fee, activate() can never pass; loosen the check and the final claimants revert.

FIX: the row is split. Fee-on-transfer is supported for streams and unsupported
for the airdrop, stated in the airdrop's contract-level NatSpec. See the
corrected table in PART 7.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C-6  MEDIUM. totalAllocated was never bound to the sum of the tree.
     blueprint lines 151 and 186. Fixed in tooling/build-tree.mjs.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

totalAllocated is immutable and activate() checks the balance against it, but
nothing tied it to the actual sum of leaf amounts. If the tree sums to more than
totalAllocated, activate() passes, early claimants succeed, and late claimants
revert on an empty balance. That is exactly the failure the funding design was
introduced to prevent, so activate() would have been a false solvency signal.

FIX: build-tree.mjs derives the root and totalAllocated from the SAME pass over
recipients.csv and writes deploy-args.json. The deploy reads that file; the
figure is never hand-typed. test_DeployArgsMatchTheTree pins the two artefacts
together, and test_Claim_AllRecipientsFullyPaid_NoDustStranded proves the sum of
full claims empties the contract to exactly zero.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C-7  MEDIUM. Duplicate leaf indices were not validated off-chain.
     blueprint line 281. Fixed in tooling/build-tree.mjs.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The script was specified to reject duplicate addresses, zero addresses and zero
amounts. It was not specified to reject duplicate INDEX. Since claimedAmount is
keyed by index alone, two leaves sharing an index cross-contaminate: recipient
A's claim advances the counter that gates recipient B, and B's payout is computed
against A's accrual.

FIX: uniqueness on index is enforced, and indices must additionally be contiguous
from zero, so they cannot silently drift from row order. Verified against seven
malformed inputs, all rejected: duplicate index, duplicate address, zero amount,
zero address, non-contiguous index, amount exceeding uint128, malformed address.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C-8  LOW to MEDIUM. Dead and duplicated state.
     blueprint lines 156 and 182, 42. Removed.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

claimedBitMap was declared and then disclaimed in the same document: "the bitmap
is only useful if you also want an O(1) has-this-index-ever-claimed view". Under
vesting an index is claimed repeatedly, so a boolean bit is not an authority over
anything. It is a second source of truth for a fact the cumulative mapping
already holds, which is a divergence waiting to happen, and it costs an extra
SSTORE on the first claim of every index.

REMOVED. "Has ever claimed" is `claimedAmount[index] != 0`.

IVestingSchedule.sol was declared in the architecture tree and never defined. An
interface no contract implements is a file that compiles and does nothing. The
two products have genuinely different shapes (a stream is addressed by id, a
claim by index plus proof), so forcing a shared interface would be contortion
rather than abstraction.

REMOVED, deliberately, rather than written. The frontend types are generated
from the ABIs.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C-9  LOW. Assorted, all closed.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Minimum duration was recommended for streams only (line 219). Now enforced for
  both, via VestingMath.MIN_DURATION = 1 hour in validateSchedule. Over an hour
  or more, a validator nudging block.timestamp by seconds moves the payout by a
  negligible fraction; below it, timestamp manipulation becomes material.

  No pragma or pinned dependency version anywhere. Now solc 0.8.28 exactly and
  OpenZeppelin pinned to v5.6.1. SafeERC20 semantics shifted across 4.x to 5.x
  (safeApprove removed in favour of forceApprove), so an unpinned import is a
  real supply-chain and correctness risk.

  uint64(block.timestamp) cast was never mentioned. Now explicit, and safe until
  year 2554.

  withdraw(id, 0) meaning "withdraw everything" was an ambiguous API that
  collides with rejecting zero-amount withdrawals. Replaced with an explicit
  withdrawMax(id). No magic zero.

  Two decisions the blueprint left open (lines 119 and 125) are now resolved and
  documented in NatSpec at the function. See PART 4.

  EVM version. The blueprint did not state one. foundry.toml pins evm_version =
  "paris", which avoids PUSH0 and transient storage. BOT Chain's EVM level is
  unverified; this is the conservative floor. If the chain is confirmed
  Shanghai or later, raising it is a one-line change and a redeploy.
  ReentrancyGuard is used rather than ReentrancyGuardTransient for the same
  reason, since the latter requires EIP-1153.

================================================================================
PART 4. DESIGN DECISIONS RESOLVED
================================================================================

The blueprint deferred two decisions with "decide and document". Both are now
decided, and the reasoning lives in NatSpec at the function so it does not drift
from the code.

4.1 MAY `start` BE IN THE PAST? Yes.

Backdating is a real payroll case: work began before the stream was set up. It
costs the sender nothing they did not choose to commit, because the deposit is
pulled in full at creation either way. The backdated portion simply becomes
claimable immediately. Backdating past `end` degenerates to a transfer with extra
steps, which is harmless.

4.2 WHO MAY CALL withdraw()? Anyone.

Funds always go to the stored recipient, so a third party can only pay gas on
the recipient's behalf. This enables gasless UX via a relayer and mirrors the
airdrop's claim-for-another property, which is the same argument the blueprint
already makes at line 180 for claims. Verified by
test_Withdraw_AnyoneMayTrigger_FundsGoToRecipient: recipient is paid, relayer
receives nothing.

4.3 IS activate() PERMISSIONED? No, deliberately.

It stores no role and grants no authority. The only state it can reach is
funded = true, and only when the balance covers every leaf. Anyone calling early
simply reverts. Adding an owner here to gate a function that cannot be abused
would reintroduce exactly the trust the design removes.

The concern that a third party could donate tokens to force premature activation
is answered by C-6, not by access control: with totalAllocated derived from the
same pass that built the root, reaching the threshold IS full solvency.

4.4 SHOULD STREAMS BE ENUMERABLE ON-CHAIN? No.

A mapping(address => uint256[]) for sender and recipient would cost an SSTORE
per side on every create to serve a frontend query. The events are indexed on
streamId, sender and recipient, which is what an indexer consumes.

This is a real trade-off, not a free win, and it is the one open operational
risk in the frontend: public RPCs commonly cap getLogs block ranges, so "My
streams" needs either a light indexer or paginated log scanning anchored at the
deployment block. Flagged in PART 9.

THE ONLY UNCOVERED BRANCHES, AND WHY THEY STAY

  src/PaymentStream.sol:228        if (vested > deposit) revert ...
  src/MerkleVestedAirdrop.sol:122  if (vested > amount) revert ...

These two are unreachable by construction and that is deliberate. They are the
non-tautological replacement for the accounting assert the blueprint asked for
at line 136 (see C-4). They cost three comparisons and they convert a silent
cross-stream drain into a revert if VestingMath ever regresses.

They are the only intentionally unreachable lines in src/. Calling them out
explicitly is the honest position: if you would rather have zero unreachable
code than a defence against a library regression, delete both lines and the two
error declarations. The recommendation is to keep them, because C-1 was exactly
a case where accrual silently exceeded what backed it.

script/Deploy.s.sol carries two more of the same kind, added later and listed
here so the count stays honest: UnexpectedInitialState, which fires if a freshly
deployed PaymentStream does not read back empty, and DeployedStateMismatch, which
fires if an immutable does not read back as the value it was given. Both are
unreachable against correct contracts and a correct compiler. They exist because
the alternative to failing loudly at that moment is a wrong address being
recorded, announced and then funded. Four intentionally unreachable branches in
the whole tree, all defensive, all named.

6.4 CLAIMS CHECKED AGAINST PINNED OPENZEPPELIN 5.6.1 SOURCE

Rather than rely on recollection of the API, three blueprint claims were read
out of lib/openzeppelin-contracts at the pinned tag:

  "SafeERC20 bounds returndata copying" (line 201). HOLDS. _safeTransfer issues
  call(gas(), token, 0, 0x00, 0x44, 0x00, 0x20), so the output buffer is 32
  bytes and a return bomb is never copied on the success path.

  Nuance the blueprint does not mention: on the FAILURE path with bubbling
  enabled, SafeERC20 does returndatacopy the full returndata to re-throw the
  reason. A hostile token that REVERTS with megabytes can therefore still charge
  the caller memory-expansion gas. The transaction reverts anyway, so this
  grieves only the caller who chose to interact with that token. Not exploitable
  here; worth knowing.

  "This is the OpenZeppelin / Uniswap standard" for double-hashed leaves
  (line 168). HOLDS, and OZ's own MerkleProof header carries the matching
  warning: "You should avoid using leaf values that are 64 bytes long prior to
  hashing ... the concatenation of a sorted pair of internal nodes could be
  reinterpreted as a leaf value."

  Internal nodes use sorted-pair hashing via Hashes.commutativeKeccak256, which
  is what the off-chain generator must match. StandardMerkleTree does, which is
  why it is the tool used.

6.5 CROSS-LANGUAGE PARITY

The blueprint calls a mismatch between the generator's leaf encoding and the
contract's "the single most likely way this project breaks" (line 286). It is
therefore tested as a real round trip, not as a Solidity re-implementation of
the same encoding checked against itself.

build-tree.mjs writes proofs.json. EncodingParityTest reads that exact file,
deploys MerkleVestedAirdrop with the root and totalAllocated from it, funds,
activates, and claims every entry using the emitted proofs. It asserts each
recipient receives their exact allocation and the contract is left holding zero.

    root            0x5eab0066553558831972ebb8d68adc8f95f314c42838b9ea229d92fdbdcc19c1
    totalAllocated  8000000000000000000024
    recipients      4
    round-trip      all 4 proofs verified against the root

The generator also replays every proof against its own root before writing any
file, so it cannot emit an artefact it has not itself verified.

6.6 FORGERY ATTEMPTS, ALL REJECTED

  proof for a different leaf                       InvalidProof
  inflated amount with a valid proof               InvalidProof
  substituted account with a valid proof           InvalidProof
  internal node presented as a leaf                InvalidProof

The last one is worth spelling out. test_Claim_InternalNodePresentedAsLeafReverts

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C-10  LOW. vestedOf misreported a cancelled stream. Found on testnet.
      Fixed in src/PaymentStream.sol vestedOf().
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This one is not a blueprint finding. It was introduced by this implementation and
found by looking at real output on a real chain, which is the honest place to
record it.

cancel() settles a stream by writing deposit = withdrawn = vested and deliberately
leaves `end` in the future, because moving `end` is exactly what caused C-1. A
cancelled stream is therefore fully settled. But vestedOf recomputed accrual from
the settled deposit, and since `end` is still ahead, it returned a FRACTION of it:
on the live stream, 16,203,703,703,703,703 against a settled deposit of
138,888,888,888,888,888.

No funds were ever at risk. claimableOf already special-cased cancellation and
returned 0, and both withdraw paths check `cancelled` before touching the
arithmetic. That check is load-bearing rather than defensive tidiness: without it
`_vested(s) - s.withdrawn` would underflow into a panic on a cancelled stream.

The impact was display. 9.4 specifies pairing vestedOf with deposit to draw the
vesting bar, so a fully settled stream rendered as barely started.

FIX: vestedOf returns `s.cancelled ? s.deposit : _vested(s)`, making it the third
view to agree that a cancelled stream is finished.

PROOF: test_VestedOf_OnCancelledStreamReadsAsFullySettled asserts equality with
the settled deposit immediately after cancellation and again a year past the
original end. The test was confirmed non-vacuous by reverting the fix, where it
fails with 111,111,111,111,111,111,111 against 333,333,333,333,333,333,333, a
third of the settled deposit and exactly the fractional recompute. Verified again
on chain after redeploying: settled deposit and vestedOf both read
138,888,888,888,888,888.

8.2 DEPLOY SCRIPTS

script/Deploy.s.sol. Two script contracts over a shared Deployment base.

    DeployPaymentStream        no arguments, no off-chain dependency
    DeployMerkleVestedAirdrop  reads tooling/out/deploy-args.json

    forge script script/Deploy.s.sol:DeployPaymentStream \
        --rpc-url $BOT_RPC_URL --account deployer --broadcast --verify

    AIRDROP_TOKEN=0x... AIRDROP_START=<unix> AIRDROP_CLIFF=<unix> \
    AIRDROP_END=<unix> \
    forge script script/Deploy.s.sol:DeployMerkleVestedAirdrop \
        --rpc-url $BOT_RPC_URL --account deployer --broadcast --verify

SHAPE. Each script separates run(), which reads the environment, from deploy(),
which takes every input explicitly and holds all the logic. The first reason was
found the hard way: environment variables are process-global and shared across
forge's concurrently running test contracts, so a suite that configures itself by
mutating them races against itself. Four tests failed non-deterministically
before the split, and they failed on the harness rather than on anything real,
which is the worst kind of red. Explicit parameters also let one script compose
another without going through the shell.

WHY THE PREFLIGHT IS LONG. Neither contract has an owner, a pause or an upgrade
path, so the deploy transaction is the last moment anything can be corrected.
Every check that CAN run before the broadcast does, because a constructor revert
on a live chain costs real gas and reports an opaque failure, while the same
revert in the script costs nothing and names the parameter.

    ExampleRecipientList     tooling/recipients.csv is still the worked example
                             of 8.1. A contract funded against that root is
                             unclaimable and unrecoverable forever. Override
                             with ALLOW_EXAMPLE_RECIPIENTS=1.
    TokenNotAContract        token is immutable and there is no sweep, so a
                             mistyped address is permanent. Code at the address
                             is a weak check, but it catches a typo.
    MainnetNotConfirmed      chain 677 requires CONFIRM_MAINNET=1. Deploying to
                             mainnet when testnet 968 was meant is not
                             retractable.
    ValueExceedsUint64       envUint returns uint256. A silent narrowing would
                             write a schedule nobody asked for into an immutable.
    EmptyTree                empty root, total or recipient count in the
                             artefact.
    ScheduleAlreadyFinished  an end date already past makes every allocation
                             claimable at once.
    InvalidSchedule          VestingMath's own validation, reached before gas is
                             spent rather than as an opaque on-chain revert.

The root and totalAllocated are never passed by hand. They come from
deploy-args.json, which puts C-6 at the point of deployment rather than only in
the generator.

READBACK. After the broadcast the script reads every immutable back off the
deployed contract and compares it against what it was given. This cannot undo a
bad deployment. What it does is stop a wrong one from being recorded, announced
and then funded, which is the point at which the mistake becomes permanent.

RECORDS. deployments/<chainId>-<contract>.json, one file per contract per chain,
so a second deployment never has to merge into the first. Written only under
--broadcast, so dry runs and the test suite leave the tree clean. That is what
lets the tests exercise the scripts directly instead of a stripped-down copy.

The record's `fromBlock` is a LOWER BOUND on the deployment block, not the block
the transaction landed in: it is captured during simulation, one block before the
deploy is mined. "My streams" anchors its paginated log scan there (9.4), and a
lower bound is the safe direction to be wrong in. The exact receipt is in
broadcast/ if it is ever needed.

fs_permissions in foundry.toml grants read on ./tooling/out and read-write on
./deployments. Nothing else.

VERIFICATION. test/Deploy.t.sol, 16 tests, calling the scripts themselves rather
than a re-implementation. The central one deploys from the generator's real
artefacts, funds, activates, claims every proof in proofs.json and asserts the
contract empties to exactly zero. A stale root or a narrowed schedule value would
not sum.

Both scripts were additionally run end to end against a local anvil node at chain
id 968: both contracts deployed, the ExampleRecipientList guard refused a real
broadcast, claim before activate() reverted, and after funding, activating and
warping past end, recipient 0 was paid exactly 1000000000000000000007 against the
generator's own proof. That exercises the broadcast path, the record writing and
the artefact round trip together. No unit test reaches any of the three.

PART 9. FRONTEND AND UI STYLE
================================================================================

Stack per blueprint: React, Vite, viem, Reown AppKit, Tailwind, Framer Motion.

9.1 THE RULE THAT OUTRANKS THE OTHERS

Never let the UI make an authorization decision. Show state, disable buttons for
UX, but every constraint is enforced on-chain. A check that exists only in the
client does not exist. Every guard in PART 2 is in the contract for this reason.

9.2 THE PER-SECOND COUNTER

The blueprint is right that this is the demo: watching a claimable balance rise
in real time sells the primitive faster than any explanation.

One correctness note. Anchor it to the last block timestamp plus locally elapsed
time, NOT to Date.now() directly, and clamp the displayed value to
deposit - withdrawn. If the browser clock runs ahead of chain time the UI shows
a number the contract will reject, and the withdraw reverts. That is the same
client-versus-chain divergence 9.1 warns about, arriving through the back door.

Use tabular numerals on every amount:

    font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums;

Without it the digits jitter as they tick, which is the difference between the
counter reading as a live instrument and reading as broken.

9.3 STYLE, DERIVED FROM Universal.txt

Universal.txt is a negative specification, so the positive style is inferred
from what it forbids.

PALETTE. Muted charcoal ground, desaturated green as the single accent. No pure
white anywhere, so the lightest surface is an off-white with a green or warm-grey
cast. No purple, no neon, no rainbow, no unthemed pastels. The consequence worth
internalising: hierarchy is carried by VALUE and SPACING, not by hue. Surfaces
separate by luminance steps.

DEPTH. No glassmorphism, no harsh gradients, no radial orbs, no dot grids, no
coloured left-border stripes on cards. Flat surfaces, hairline borders,
restrained shadow. A card's identity comes from its border and background value.

ICONS. Not Lucide. Phosphor, Remix Icon, Tabler, or custom SVG. Phosphor pairs
best with a muted palette because of its lighter default stroke weight.

TYPE. Inter is explicitly allowed; keep it. Tabular numerals on all figures.

COPY. No em dashes in UI copy or headings. No "it's not X, it's Y" constructions.
No fake testimonials or placeholder social proof; omit the section entirely if
real content is not available.

LAYOUT. No bento grid unless content hierarchy actually calls for it. Bottom
navigation on mobile. For this app: Streams, Create, Airdrop, Account.

ONE APPARENT CONFLICT, RESOLVED. The blueprint specifies Framer Motion and makes
the ticking counter the centrepiece. That is compatible with the anti-pattern
list, because the constraint is on DECORATIVE motion, not functional motion.
Keep the counter animated. Skip page-entrance flourishes.

9.4 SCREENS

    Create stream    form, approve, create, with a live schedule preview
    My streams       incoming and outgoing tabs
    Stream detail    vesting bar, per-second counter, withdraw, cancel if
                     permitted
    Airdrop claim    connect, eligibility lookup, vested versus locked, claim

Contract views that back these: vestedOf(id) paired with streams(id).deposit
draws the vesting bar; claimableOf(id) is what the counter interpolates between;
the airdrop's claimableOf(index, amount) previews a claim without a proof,
because showing a number to an unproven caller releases nothing.

OPERATIONAL RISK, from 4.4. RETIRED, measured rather than budgeted for. "My
streams" reads indexed events, and public RPCs commonly cap getLogs block ranges.
This node does not: an address-filtered query across 20M blocks, the entire chain,
returns in about 1.5 seconds. No indexer and no pagination. The scan is still
anchored at the deployment block from the deploy record, because that is free.
See 6.7 for the measurement, including the unfiltered numbers that would have
been a problem.

9.5 APPROVE FLOW

create() requires a prior approval. USDT-style tokens revert on a non-zero to
non-zero approve, so the frontend must zero the allowance first or use a
forceApprove-equivalent path. This is a frontend concern, not a contract one,
and it is the single most common cause of a failed first transaction.


9.6 WHAT WAS BUILT

web/. React 18, Vite, TypeScript, viem, Tailwind, Framer Motion. 3,319 lines.
Typecheck clean, production build clean.

    npm run dev         vite dev server
    npm run build       typecheck then production bundle
    npm run verify      accrual mirror against the deployed contract
    npm run sync-abi    regenerate ABIs from the forge artifacts

WALLET CONNECTION. EIP-6963 multi-wallet discovery with a window.ethereum
fallback, rather than the Reown AppKit the blueprint names. AppKit needs a
projectId from a hosted account, and 6963 needs nothing, works in exactly the
in-app wallet browsers PART 10 is about, and is what those browsers inject anyway.
Desktop QR-to-mobile pairing is the tradeoff. AppKit can be layered on later
without touching the screens.

THE ACCRUAL MIRROR IS THE RISK, AND IT IS TESTED LIKE ONE

src/lib/vesting.ts is a second implementation of VestingMath, in another language,
whose output the UI presents as if it were the contract's answer. That is the same
shape of hazard PART 8 calls the single most likely way this project breaks for
the Merkle encoding, and it is answered the same way rather than by inspection.

scripts/verify-vesting-parity.ts replays the DEPLOYED contract's vestedOf and
claimableOf against the mirror at blocks sampled across each stream's whole
lifetime. Both sides are evaluated AT THE SAME BLOCK, so the comparison is exact
instead of racing the clock. The sample window is derived per stream from its
StreamCreated log, because sampling before a stream existed silently skips and
would overstate how much was actually compared.

    streams checked   3
    comparisons       144
    mismatches        0

The three streams on chain cover the cliff, streaming, terminal and cancelled
branches between them, so C-10's fix is inside the compared surface.

THE COUNTER. Anchored to the last block timestamp plus performance.now() elapsed,
never Date.now(), and re-synced on an interval and whenever the tab becomes
visible again, since a backgrounded tab stops firing timers and returns with a
stale anchor. performance.now() is monotonic, so a user whose clock is wrong, or
which changes under them, still sees chain time. claimableOf clamps to
deposit - withdrawn, so the displayed figure can never exceed what the contract
will pay.

AUTHORISATION. Per 9.1, no constraint lives only in the client. Buttons disable
for clarity, and every one of those conditions is separately enforced on chain.
Contract reverts are surfaced by their custom error name rather than as "execution
reverted", because each of those errors was chosen to say precisely what was
wrong, and discarding that would leave the user guessing.

STYLE, against the negative specification in Universal.txt. Charcoal ground, one
desaturated green accent, and one muted clay reserved for destructive actions.
Hierarchy is carried by luminance and spacing, as 9.3 predicted it would have to
be. No pure white, no sparkles, no glassmorphism, no gradients, no orbs or dot
grids, no coloured left-border stripes, no bento grid, no Lucide (icons are custom
SVG), no em dashes in copy. Every figure renders through one Amount component, so
tabular numerals are not something anyone has to remember to apply. Motion is
functional only: the bar transition and a pulse marking the counter as live. No
page-entrance flourishes.

KNOWN GAP. The bundle is 594 kB, 185 kB gzipped, almost entirely viem. Acceptable
on desktop, less so over mobile data inside a wallet browser. A code-split pass is
the fix and has not been done.

THE ROBUST PATTERN. Below roughly 640px, and in any wallet WebView, replace every
dropdown with a full-width BOTTOM SHEET rendered into a portal at the document
root. It sidesteps all four causes at once, gives a larger tap target, and sits
naturally with the bottom navigation Universal.txt already asks for. On desktop
the same component renders as a conventional anchored popover.

Detect by capability, not by user-agent string:

    const isInjectedWalletBrowser =
      typeof window !== "undefined" &&
      !!window.ethereum &&
      /Mobi|Android/i.test(navigator.userAgent);

Apply the sheet variant when that is true OR the viewport is narrow.

Affected controls in this app: the token selector on Create, the duration
presets, and the incoming/outgoing tab switcher on My streams.

Note that glassmorphism is already banned by Universal.txt on aesthetic grounds.
That ban happens to also remove backdrop-filter, which is unreliable in these
same WebViews. The style rule and the compatibility fix agree here.

IMPLEMENTED, all four causes, in web/:

    native <select>        none in the app. Every dropdown is Select in
                           components/Sheet.tsx, built from buttons.
    transform ancestors    components/Portal.tsx renders sheets into document.body.

                           every animation, so this is not hypothetical here.
    100vh                  --app-height in index.css is 100dvh behind an @supports

Detection is by capability, as specified: isInjectedWalletBrowser() in
lib/wallet.ts tests for an injected provider plus a mobile user agent, and the
sheet variant is applied on that OR a narrow viewport, so a desktop browser at a
small window size gets the same treatment.

  every EVM level. 6.7 has the measured cost of that choice, about 1,930 gas per
  protected call. A mainnet RPC URL settles it in seconds.

  FRONTEND GAPS. Built (9.6), but: the bundle is 594 kB / 185 kB gzipped, almost
  all viem, and wants a code-split pass before it is served over mobile data; and
  wallet connection is EIP-6963 only, so desktop users without an extension have
  no QR-to-mobile path. The airdrop screen is complete but shows a "no
  distribution is live yet" state until the contract above exists.

  THE FRONTEND HAS NO AUTOMATED UI TESTS. The accrual mirror is verified against
  the deployed contract (9.6) because that is where a divergence would silently
  mislead a user. The screens themselves have been exercised by hand only, and
  the wallet-browser remedies in PART 10 have not been tested on a physical
  device inside a real in-app browser.

  NO EXTERNAL AUDIT. 83 passing tests and 100% line coverage are evidence, not a
  substitute. The four intentionally unreachable defensive branches, two in src/
  and two in script/, are documented in PART 5 so an auditor does not have to
  rediscover why they are there.


================================================================================
PART 13. SUMMARY
================================================================================

The blueprint's architecture is sound and its central design argument, that
revocability must differ between the two products and that non-revocability has
to be structural rather than configurable, is correct and worth defending under
questioning.

Its security specification was mostly right. The token compatibility table, the
double-hash leaf encoding, the decision to pay the leaf's account rather than
the caller, and the refusal to add any escape hatch are all correct, and two of
those were verified against pinned OpenZeppelin source rather than taken on
trust.

Four things were wrong, in descending order of consequence.

The specified cancellation logic drains the contract. Setting end =
block.timestamp to freeze accrual makes VestingMath report the full deposit as
vested forever after, letting a cancelled stream's recipient withdraw the
sender's already-refunded remainder out of other streams' balances. This is
demonstrated by a passing test against a model of the original design, not
asserted. The fix is to settle the deposit down to what accrued and never touch
end.

The spec contradicted itself on whether cancel() pays the recipient, which would
have sent an implementer down one of two incompatible paths with a test matrix
matching neither.

The shared library performed unchecked arithmetic while trusting its callers to
have validated the schedule, in the one component the document itself calls the
single point of failure. Reordering three guards makes it total across the entire
input space at no gas cost and with no reliance on any caller.

The on-chain accounting assert reduced to deposit == deposit. It cost gas,
verified nothing, and would not have caught the critical bug it was positioned
to catch.

All four are fixed, along with six lower-severity findings. The result is 438
lines of source across three contracts, 83 passing tests, 100% line and function
coverage, zero compiler and zero linter warnings across src/, script/ and test/,
and no dead code: every custom
error is reachable, every event is emitted, every import is referenced, every
entrypoint is exercised. Four branches in the tree are unreachable on purpose,
two in src/ and two in the deploy script. All four are defensive guards that
convert a silent wrong result into a halt, and all four are called out by file
and line in PART 5 so the decision to keep them stays the reader's to make.

The remaining risk is concentrated in three places, none of them in the accrual
math: the EVM version assumed for BOT Chain, the RPC log-range limits that "My
streams" depends on, and the wallet-browser rendering issue, which is understood
and has a specific remedy in PART 10.

Deployment is now scripted rather than manual, and the script is where the
irreversibility of these contracts is taken seriously: no owner, no pause and no
sweep means the deploy transaction is the last moment anything can be corrected,
so every check that can run before the broadcast runs before it, and the
immutables are read back off the chain afterwards. The guard most likely to
matter refuses to deploy the placeholder recipient list, which would otherwise
produce a funded contract that nobody can claim from and nobody can unwind.

PaymentStream is live and verified on testnet 968, and the live lifecycle
confirmed on chain what the suite asserted: cancellation conserved value to the
wei, the contract settled to exactly zero, and C-1 stayed fixed past the point
where the blueprint's version would have re-exposed the sender's refund. Two of
the three risks this document ended on were open only because nobody had asked
the chain, and asking cost nothing: the getLogs limit that would have forced an
indexer does not exist for address-filtered queries, and testnet's EVM level is
Cancun. Mainnet's is still unknown, which is why paris remains the target.

Live testing also produced the one finding this implementation introduced rather
than inherited. C-10 is small and cost nobody anything, but it is worth reading
as a pattern: cancel() leaves `end` in the future on purpose because moving it
caused C-1, and vestedOf had not been taught that consequence. A deliberate fix
in one place left a stale assumption in another, and no test caught it because
every test asked whether the money was right, which it was. Looking at real
output on a real chain is what surfaced it.

Blueprint build-order steps 1 through 7 are now complete for PaymentStream. Step
5's contract is written and tested but cannot be deployed until a real recipient
list exists, which is a business input rather than remaining work.
